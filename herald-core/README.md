# `eclips-herald-core`

> The pure transition composes local controlled
> publication, structural sequencing/reconciliation, generation-qualified vector
> and topology-cut progress, continuing alignment, all eight EAPP operations,
> and Start/End administration. Label, deletion, and explicit process termination
> execute through the live Oracle, EPRP, and whole-Herald owners described by the
> [label design](../docs/architecture/labels.md). Generation-qualified
> peer recovery adds local suspicion; voter-host failure probes, Oracle-ordered
> non-voter retirement, permanent fencing, terminal retired-source repair, exact
> Graph-owner successor-base installation, and irreversible local isolation
> execute through the live transition and protocol boundaries.
> Focused properties and real-TCP crash/partition tests exercise these paths.
> The transition also includes retained direct `newenv`, whole-
> manifest structural settlement, and checked application access through the
> sole current RPC/API boundary. The live disappearance leaf composes local-take
> initiation, captured-membership cuts, checked absence evidence, ordered
> publication and alignment markers, and stable Oracle requests. A shared
> package-private controlled-removal applicator serves live label deletion and
> disappearance resolution through distinct checked authorities. It
> installs payload-free Controlled suppression plus an incarnation-independent
> Store purge ledger, removes owner-local payload, possession, and topology
> fallout, completes affected waits with or without a live delivery, and pins
> fresh work at `4 + F` with zero-work exact replay. The sole live Oracle/EORC
> schema contains Open, Report, Invalidate, Resolve, and authorized Abort;
> EPRP carries the exact probe markers. The regular-retirement transaction removes
> one exact dynamic SortRegistry occurrence, installs cut/control-qualified Store
> suppression without rewriting raw history, and permits only the equal
> Resolve-derived successor occurrence. The enclosing predefined `sort-sort`
> carrier remains in its primordial occurrence; the new epoch belongs only to the
> regular sort defined by its payload. Nabla/Delta carriers likewise keep their
> primordial outer occurrence and use a retained control floor to bind an embedded
> regular `sort_id`. Fence-held work preserves its exact outer sort/occurrence and
> that combined floor. Stale peer carriers advance only transcript/vector
> structural progress and create no live semantic projection. On
> an active destination, stale alignment revisions block until every exact
> destination/evidence-kind application is terminally ignored; on an active
> source they block until acknowledgement covers their base/change revision, and
> cancellation settles only that side. The raw entries remain immutable contiguous
> transcript after becoming inert. Epoch-projected alignment snapshots and
> observation-only or effective-only revisioned changes keep a valid lower-ID
> successor recoverable without rewriting raw audit snapshots. If every incumbent
> alignment-plan coordinate for retained structural debt is invalidated, the debt
> becomes live again and both spontaneous work and anchor replay choose the earliest
> matching installed cut at or after the latest incumbent topology for their selected
> placement vector. This allows same-topology recovery with a fresh vector but not an
> exact tombstoned-vector jump. Promotion receipts, superseded routes, and historical
> certificates remain immutable; only uncovered debt, current routes, and current
> non-invalidated certificates remain live blockers. Whole-state
> validation rejects live stale dependency evidence and checks that the frozen
> Open cut covers every matching local definition position. The composed path
> proves eligibility with an application-visible definition and exact
> `local-take`; fresh work is exactly `4 + W + F`, and terminal replay is inert.
> The [verification guide](../docs/verification/README.md) describes the
> property, process, boundary and source-archive checks.

This package is the home of the composed pure Herald kernel and its leaf state
owners, including local and remote prepared-child lifecycle. The boundary
contains the checked startup path, application value/query/descriptor translation, logical
sessions with retained requests and stable reply cursors, local
publication/store/wait ownership, and the eight executable regular calls inside
the pure `stepHerald` transition. It also composes logical peer discovery,
sequenced placement
projections, immutable ordinary/structural peer batches, resumable streams,
ordered disappearance markers, label-installation reports, structural and
alignment controls, and
pure dispatch attempts into the same state transition. Narrow application, administration, and
peer RPC adapters translate structurally decoded claims at the typed input/effect
boundary. The Oracle client retains both its fixed-contact contiguous watch and
exact semantic-intent request/result workflows for dynamic Start, explicit
End, label decision/completion, failure probes, and disappearance; ordinary controlled
publication and `newenv` remain direct.

The private state product includes local controlled lifecycle, suppression, and
possession; retry-stable generic publication and forward; unsequenced and stamped
structural stages; deterministic reconciliation; installed vector/cut progress;
cause-qualified alignment debts; dynamic process identity/scaffolds and selected
primordial grants; retained local child preparation, initial claim, cancellation
and own-End outcomes; and
the live LabelBarrier, Visibility, and Disappearance leaves. Those owners are
reachable only through the public typed transition and its RPC facades, never by downstream
mutation. Controlled peer observation, Store-derived possession through
`read`/`local-take`, label settlement, and resident process retirement remain
part of the same atomic owner product. Peer liveness is another pure leaf: exact
binding loss, or the first owner-authorized attempt to contact a known unbound
peer, arms one absolute generation-qualified timer. The exact current Hello
cancels it, stale outcomes are inert, and expiry produces local suspicion only.

The live label path settles selected publication-source groups and endpoint
holds before submitting one exact Oracle decision. The Oracle fixes the semantic
outcome atomically. Each captured Herald installs a successful outcome directly
through the checked owners, retains its terminal evidence and sends a
`LabelInstalled` report to the collector. The collector offers one
membership-qualified completion after the required surviving installations are
present. `LabelBarrier` owns historical installation evidence and collection;
it does not close a global observation gate or run a separate preparation phase.
NotApplied decisions do not start installation collection. The
[label architecture](../docs/architecture/labels.md) specifies the admission,
installation and return conditions.

Label release and process End use the same effective-publication, historical
authority, normalized topology-consequence, and alignment owners as ordinary
structural progress. Nabla release retires matching pristine reservations; End
retires the exact resident process state and turns its effective process labels
into zombie labels. Socket or application-session loss never synthesizes End.
Destination-incarnation loss discards its alignment obligation, while selected
source-incarnation loss recomputes that obligation against the surviving source.

The `PeerDispatch` facade gives a shell abstract owner-minted ticket and attempt
types plus the constructible `Written | Deferred | Failed` outcome it must report
through `RuntimeObservation`. The shell can return correlations it received in
effects but cannot mint or alter them. `Peer.RPC` is the sole exhaustive bridge
that may inspect an incoming peer item, reconstruct checked owner values, validate
its semantic digest, or project an owner-minted publication attempt.

Genesis is checked in two stages. `checkHeraldGenesis` validates and canonicalizes
the deployment-wide membership, closed predefined catalogue, primordial

Oracle genesis fact. `checkInitialBootstraps` selects a coherent global set of those

also resolves a fixture-only symbolic universal-edge manifest. Both contribute to
one canonical `InitialProjectionDigest`. Peer compatibility
terminates on a digest mismatch; initialization itself installs only the processes
whose checked residence is the local Herald epoch. Every Herald also creates six
private system-view stores, including the primordial `sort-sort` publications,
even when the process selection is empty.

`initialHerald` consumes checked genesis, checked initial bootstraps, an initial
monotonic instant, one checked non-empty `OracleContactSet`, one mandatory
checked `GeneratorSeed`, and checked application- and peer-recovery
configurations. It returns
either a closed invariant fault or the fully composed opaque serving

No leaf successor or effect becomes observable unless the complete transition has
been prepared successfully and the composed invariant holds.

`Prepared` packages a complete successor and output after every semantic decision
has succeeded. Failure exposes no successor, and commit merely reveals the already
complete result: it performs no semantic validation and has no semantic failure
branch. Each leaf-specific preparer is responsible for its successor invariant and
output invariant. The generic plan is ephemeral implementation machinery, never replay,
persistence, or protocol data.

The application leaf's private identity owner keeps one bidirectional
`PrivateUniqueId`/`GlobalUniqueId` map per explicitly registered live
`ProcessEpochId`. Unknown and retired epochs cannot localize or resolve IDs.
Localization is stable and counters are independent between process epochs.
Resident startup installation also records one opaque application attachment and

Logical sessions share the process map and startup access while retaining distinct
epoch-qualified session IDs, resume tokens, binding generations, and live nonce
retry keys. `EndSession` removes only session-scoped state; binding loss preserves the

process-epoch tombstone, preventing stale private values from gaining new meaning
if a retired epoch is presented again. The private counter starts at one; zero is
the application type's structural sentinel, not a security boundary.

The separate EAPP lifecycle envelope leaves the eight-operation union unchanged.

overrides replay. The client completes that Resume handoff before exposing startup
access, including for an idle application.

Parent and explicit EADM cancellation use the same serialized claim/cancel winner.

Preparation, child lifetime and accepted work survive parent End. Own-process End

Application values can recursively contain private IDs.
Inbound translation admits textual record fields through the semantic domain,
resolves every private leaf without allocating, and validates private process
labels through an immutable applied-Oracle process-role projection that includes
remote process epochs. Outbound ordinary-value translation prepares all fresh
aliases and the fully localized value together, visiting checked record fields in
ascending order. The primitive is deliberately not a sort-definition recognizer;
the use-case coordinator routes checked definitions through their dedicated

invalid field text, and false process-role claims are ordinary rejections;
private-map lifecycle or checked internal-label contradictions remain separately
typed invariant faults for the owning coordinator to terminate on.

Every Herald has exactly one pure generated-identity owner. Its
32-byte seed is an AES-256 key; the private owner derives the documented
zero-excluding 24-byte prefix and applies the exact unpadded two-block CBC
permutation to the prefix/counter package. For one fixed key, different packages
therefore produce different 256-bit IDs, and the chosen prefix makes the all-zero
ID unreachable. Preparation is repeatable and commit advances the 64-bit counter
once. The prototype performs no retained-ID collision scan, prefix rotation,
rekeying, rollover handling, or entropy acquisition inside the kernel.

generated global ID, the caller process's next private alias, the generator and

The reservation creates no controlled-object role, possession, value, store,
